Teaching a computer to name every pixel

When you look at a photo you instantly see a person, a road and the sky. A computer only sees a grid of tiny colored dots. Semantic segmentation is how it learns to say what each dot belongs to.

1A photo is a grid of dots

Each dot is called a pixel. It has a color but no meaning. The computer does not know it is part of a tree.

2A trained model names each dot

The model practiced on thousands of photos where people had already colored every dot by hand, until it could do it on new photos.

3Same kind, same color

All cars share one color, all people share another. The result is a colored map of the picture called a mask.

See it on a tiny picture

In plain words: this toy picture has only 96 pixels. Switch between the two views. The camera view is just colors. The answer view gives every pixel a name: sky, sun, tree, house, grass or road. A real photo works the same way, with hundreds of thousands of pixels.

What happens inside the network

The network first shrinks the picture to understand it, then grows it back to color every pixel. Shortcuts carry small details across.

Shrink to understand, grow to draw

Run a photo in step 2 to see this with your own picture.

The U-Net shape

Click a part to highlight it. The photo and label map on the sides appear after you run a photo in step 2.

Encoder: read and summarize

Like reading a book and writing a short summary. The picture gets smaller but the meaning gets clearer.

Decoder: rewrite the story

It grows the summary back to full size and colors every pixel.

Skip connections: sticky notes

Fine details like edges are passed straight across, so the final outlines stay sharp.

In plain words: a "fully convolutional" network keeps the picture layout all the way through, so it can answer for every pixel instead of giving one answer for the whole photo. The default models are SegFormer networks trained on thousands of scene photos (ADE20K: 150 kinds such as road, building, window, grass and sky). The older FCN and DeepLabV3 options only know 20 kinds of objects, so they call everything else background.
